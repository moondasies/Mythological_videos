## Method and honesty notes

- Each record separates **what Valmiki says**, **later versions**, and **folklore**.
- Sarga numbers follow the common Southern recension, as used by valmikiramayan.net, sanskritdocuments.org and the Hari Prasad Shastri translation on wisdomlib.org. The Baroda **Critical Edition** numbers differ in places, so every reference is marked:
  - ✅ **confirmed by an online source this session**
  - 🔎 **to verify against the verse text before scripting**
- I did **not** read the full Sanskrit of every sarga. Statements marked 🔎 come from my prior knowledge of the text. They must be checked verse by verse before any script line uses them.
- The container's network blocks direct access to wisdomlib, valmikiramayan.net and similar sites, so research ran through web search only. For the selected pilot topic, I will fetch the actual sarga text once access is available (see the feasibility report).
- No verse is quoted here. Quotations are added only after they have been checked against the text.

## Research records

### 1. Trijata's dream — त्रिजटा का स्वप्न
- **Characters:** Trijata (an elderly rakshasi), Sita, the rakshasi guards; dream figures: Rama, Lakshmana, Ravana and Lanka.
- **Primary source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda, **Sarga 27** ✅ (Sita's response to the omens follows in nearby sargas 🔎).
- **Passage summary:** The rakshasis threaten Sita. Trijata wakes and tells them about a dream she has had. In it she sees Rama and Lakshmana in white garlands and white garments, and Sita in white, seated on a white mountain surrounded by the ocean ✅. My recollection, still 🔎, is that the dream also shows Ravana with a shaven head, in red, smeared with oil and drawn southward by donkeys, other rakshasas falling, Lanka sinking, and Vibhishana alone in white. Trijata reads this as Rama's victory and tells the others to stop threatening Sita and seek her favour instead.
- **Other versions:** Ramcharitmanas (Sundar Kand) includes a short Trijata dream episode 🔎. Several later traditions make Trijata **Vibhishana's daughter**. This is **not stated in Valmiki** 🔎, so the script must not say it as Valmiki fact.
- **Firmly supported:** Trijata exists, she has the dream, the dream foretells Rama's victory, and she warns the other rakshasis.
- **Uncertain:** The exact verse numbers, which differ between recensions, and the exact imagery, until checked verse by verse.
- **Later folklore:** Trijata as Vibhishana's daughter and as a long-term friend of Sita, plus regional (Southeast Asian) expansions of her role.
- **Why interesting:** Inside Ravana's own garden, one of his own rakshasis foresees his fall. It works as a hook, has emotional depth and is very visual (dream imagery in white versus red), and the cast is small.

### 2. Sampati — संपाति
- **Characters:** Sampati, Jatayu (in flashback), Angada, Hanuman, Jambavan and other vanaras; the sage Nishakara.
- **Primary source:** Kishkindha Kanda, **Sargas 56–63** ✅ (the burnt wings are told in Sargas 58 and 61 ✅; the wings regrow in Sarga 63 ✅).
- **Passage summary:** The vanaras are in despair and talk about Jatayu's death. Sampati, an old vulture without wings, hears his brother's name. Long ago the two brothers flew toward the sun. When Jatayu weakened, Sampati shielded him with his own wings, which burnt, and he fell on the Vindhya mountain ✅. With his far sight, he tells them that Sita is in Lanka. His wings then grow back, as the sage Nishakara had foretold ✅.
- **Other versions:** Many retellings call it a "race" to the sun out of pride. Details shift between sargas 58 and 61 (noon sun versus a sun as large as the earth) ✅.
- **Firmly supported:** The burnt wings, the shielding of Jatayu, the fall, the directions to Lanka and the regrown wings.
- **Uncertain:** The motive for the flight is told with different emphasis across sargas.
- **Why interesting:** A brother's sacrifice decades earlier makes the search for Sita possible. The payoff is strong, but there is a lot of story for 60 seconds.

### 3. Ahalya — अहल्या (not a stone in Valmiki)
- **Characters:** Ahalya, Gautama, Indra (implied, handled with restraint), Rama, Lakshmana, Vishwamitra.
- **Primary source:** Bala Kanda, **Sarga 48** (the curse) ✅ and **Sarga 49** (the release) 🔎.
- **Passage summary:** In Valmiki, Gautama curses Ahalya to live for thousands of years on air, without food, lying in ashes, **unseen by all beings** in the hermitage. **She is not turned into stone** ✅. When Rama arrives she becomes visible again and receives him as a guest 🔎.
- **Other versions:** The stone version (and the version where Rama's foot touches the stone) appears in later texts, including Adhyatma Ramayana and Ramcharitmanas 🔎, and possibly Raghuvamsha 🔎.
- **Firmly supported:** In Valmiki she is invisible, not stone ✅.
- **Sensitivity:** The cause of the curse is Indra's deception. That needs restrained, family-appropriate handling, and the episode must not shame Ahalya.
- **Why interesting:** It is a "what you know vs what the text says" revelation with a strong source and an emotional ending.

### 4. Kumbhakarna's slip of the tongue — कुम्भकर्ण का वरदान
- **Primary source:** Uttara Kanda, **Sarga 10** ✅.
- **Passage summary:** The gods fear the boon Kumbhakarna might ask for. At their request, Saraswati enters his speech, and he asks for long sleep ✅. The Uttara Kanda translation gives "sleep for innumerable years" ✅. The popular **"six months asleep, one day awake"** pattern is **not clearly the Valmiki wording**, and sources disagree ✅.
- **Word-play (Nirdevatva → Nidravatva):** This appears in popular retellings ✅. Whether it is in the Sanskrit verse itself is 🔎.
- **Credibility caveat:** Many scholars consider the Uttara Kanda a later layer of the text. The script should say "उत्तरकाण्ड के अनुसार".
- **Why interesting:** It is very hookable and has high relevance to the film, since Kumbhakarna is a known character.

### 5. Kabandha — कबन्ध
- **Primary source:** Aranya Kanda, **Sargas 69–73** ✅.
- **Passage summary:** A huge headless being with a mouth in his belly seizes Rama and Lakshmana. They cut off his arms. He reveals that he was cursed (by the sage Sthulashira and by Indra's vajra ✅). Once cremated, he rises in celestial form and **advises Rama to befriend Sugriva** ✅ and to go to Pampa and Shabari 🔎.
- **Why interesting:** Without this "monster" there is no alliance with Sugriva. The visuals are a challenge and could be frightening for younger viewers.

### 6. Hanuman's forgotten strength — हनुमान अपनी शक्ति क्यों भूले
- **Sources:** The curse by the sages is in Uttara Kanda **Sarga 36** (one web source ✅, still to verify 🔎). Jambavan's reminder is in Kishkindha Kanda **Sarga 66** ✅ (one source).
- **Caveat:** The well-known "curse of the rishis" is in the Uttara Kanda. In Kishkindha 66 itself, Jambavan narrates Hanuman's birth and powers 🔎.
- **Why interesting:** It is very relevant, but the story is fairly well known. The "why" angle makes it fresher.

### 7. Vedavati — वेदवती
- **Source:** Uttara Kanda, **Sarga 17** 🔎.
- **Summary:** Ravana molests Vedavati, a woman ascetic. She enters fire, vowing to be reborn to cause his destruction. The text identifies her rebirth as Sita 🔎.
- **Caveat:** It is from the Uttara Kanda and the theme is sensitive. The hook is strong.

### 8. Swayamprabha's cave — स्वयंप्रभा की गुफा
- **Source:** Kishkindha Kanda, **Sargas 50–52** ✅.
- **Summary:** The southern search party enters the dark Riksha-bila. Inside is a radiant hidden city built by Maya and given to Hema, and Swayamprabha guards it. The vanaras close their eyes, and she transports them out by her tapas ✅.
- **Why interesting:** It is visually stunning, but the plot payoff is weaker.

### 9. Mainaka — मैनाक पर्वत
- **Source:** Sundara Kanda, **Sarga 1** ✅.
- **Summary:** The Ocean asks the golden Mainaka to rise so Hanuman can rest. Mainaka owes Vayu a debt, because Vayu saved him when Indra was cutting the mountains' wings ✅. Hanuman touches the mountain and declines to rest ✅.
- **Why interesting:** Mountains once had wings, and Hanuman refuses to rest. It is visually spectacular.

### 10. Shabari — शबरी (what Valmiki describes)
- **Source:** Aranya Kanda, **Sarga 74** ✅.
- **Summary:** In Valmiki, Shabari is an aged female ascetic, a disciple of the sages of Matanga's hermitage, who honours Rama and then ascends by her own tapas. Commentators state that the **tasted-berries** detail is **not in Valmiki** ✅ and is not in Ramcharitmanas either (per one commentator ✅). The tasted-berries detail appears in later devotional traditions. Sources disagree about which text is the earliest: Adhyatma Ramayana or the Odia poet Balaram Das ✅.
- **Sensitivity:** This must be framed as "दोनों परम्पराएँ अपनी जगह सुंदर हैं" (both traditions are beautiful in their own place), not as debunking. The "earliest source" claim stays out of the script until it is verified.

### 11. Lakshmana Rekha — लक्ष्मण रेखा
- **Claim to verify:** The line is widely said to be absent from Valmiki's Aranya Kanda (Sargas 45–46 🔎) and to appear in later traditions 🔎.
- **Risk:** Saying that something is absent from a text requires a careful full-text check. The story payoff is also thin for 60 seconds.

### 12. The squirrel at the bridge — गिलहरी
- **Source:** Folk and regional tradition. It is **not in Valmiki** (Yuddha Kanda Sarga 22 has Nala building the setu 🔎).
- **Use:** It could only be told explicitly as लोक-परम्परा (folk tradition). It is low on credibility, so it does not suit the pilot.

## Top three recommendations

1. **Trijata's dream** (4.65): **recommended pilot.** It is a self-contained single scene and fits in 60 seconds. It has a small cast (Trijata, Sita, 3–4 rakshasis, and Rama, Lakshmana and Ravana as dream figures), strong white-versus-red dream imagery, a clean Valmiki source, and a natural question to open on: *a rakshasi in Ravana's own garden who saw his end.*
2. **Sampati** (4.55): the best emotional story, but it is dense for 60 seconds and needs more characters and flashback. It is a good second episode.
3. **Ahalya — not a stone** (4.50): the strongest "the text says something different" reveal. It needs careful handling of the Indra context, so it is better as episode 2 or 3, once the tone is established.
